# Adversarial attack on the scale-covariant G1* conjecture

## Candidate under attack

The scale-covariant candidate introduced in the previous audit was

\[
\Omega_\theta(t)=\{x:|\omega(x,t)|>\theta\|\omega(t)\|_{L^\infty}\},
\qquad
\rho_*(t)=\kappa\frac{\|\omega(t)\|_{L^2}}{\|\nabla\omega(t)\|_{L^2}},
\]

and

\[
|\sin\angle(\omega(x,t),\omega(y,t))|
\le C\left(\frac{|x-y|}{\rho_*(t)}\right)^{1/2}
\tag{G1*}
\]

for all relevant `x,y in Omega_theta(t)` with `|x-y|<rho_*(t)`.

This note tests whether such a statement can hold as a **universal kinematic consequence** of smoothness, incompressibility and the global scale `rho_*`.

## 1. Numerical stress test

The previous two-tube pseudo-spectral pilot was rerun across:

- resolutions `N=24,32,40`;
- viscosities `nu=0.04,0.02,0.01`;
- high-vorticity thresholds `theta=0.4,0.6,0.8`;
- additive divergence-free-projected perturbation amplitudes `0, 0.01, 0.03` in selected cases.

For `theta=0.6`, `nu=0.02`, zero perturbation, the normalized Hölder constant

\[
C_{1/2}^{\rm obs}
=\sup_{0<|x-y|<\rho_*}
\frac{|\sin\angle(\omega(x),\omega(y))|}
{\sqrt{|x-y|/\rho_*}}
\]

was approximately

- `0.07194` at `N=32`,
- `0.06917` at `N=40`.

With perturbations it increased:

- `0.08459` for `N=32`, perturbation `0.01`;
- `0.11847` for `N=32`, perturbation `0.03`;
- `0.09878` for `N=40`, perturbation `0.01`.

However, the proposed scale was only about one mesh interval:

\[
\rho_*/\Delta x\approx1.0\text{--}1.12.
\]

Therefore the apparent boundedness of `C_{1/2}` is **not numerically discriminating**. There are too few sub-`rho_*` scales to fit a genuine Hölder exponent. The extended test does not validate G1*.

## 2. Analytic adversarial packet construction

There is a more serious issue. A global scale `rho_*` cannot by itself control the local direction field on every high-amplitude component.

Take a fixed smooth divergence-free background vorticity `omega_bg` with

\[
0<c_1\le \|\omega_{bg}\|_2,
\qquad
0<c_2\le \|\nabla\omega_{bg}\|_2,
\]

so its contribution keeps `rho_*` of order one.

Choose a fixed smooth compactly supported divergence-free packet `phi` whose direction differs by an order-one angle between two points `a,b` of comparable nonzero magnitude. Such a packet can be realized as the curl of a smooth compactly supported vector potential.

For a large amplitude parameter `A`, let

\[
\ell_A=A^{-p},\qquad p>2,
\]

and define

\[
\omega_A(x)=\omega_{bg}(x)
+A\,\phi\!\left(\frac{x-x_0}{\ell_A}\right).
\]

The packet scales as

\[
\|A\phi((\cdot-x_0)/\ell_A)\|_2
\asymp A\ell_A^{3/2}
=A^{1-3p/2}\to0,
\]

while

\[
\|\nabla[A\phi((\cdot-x_0)/\ell_A)]\|_2
\asymp A\ell_A^{1/2}
=A^{1-p/2}\to0
\]

because `p>2`.

Hence the packet can have arbitrarily large pointwise vorticity while making asymptotically negligible contributions to both global norms. Consequently

\[
\rho_*(\omega_A)
=\kappa\frac{\|\omega_A\|_2}{\|\nabla\omega_A\|_2}
\to \rho_*(\omega_{bg})>0.
\]

On the other hand, the packet dominates `||omega_A||_infinity`. By choosing two points

\[
x_A=x_0+\ell_A a,
\qquad
y_A=x_0+\ell_A b,
\]

where `|phi(a)|` and `|phi(b)|` are both a fixed fraction of `||phi||_infinity`, both points belong to `Omega_theta` for a fixed admissible `theta<1` and sufficiently large `A`.

Their separation is

\[
|x_A-y_A|\asymp\ell_A,
\]

while their vorticity directions differ by an order-one angle:

\[
|\sin\angle(\omega_A(x_A),\omega_A(y_A))|\ge c_*>0.
\]

If G1* held with a universal constant `C`, it would force

\[
c_*
\le C\sqrt{\frac{\ell_A}{\rho_*(\omega_A)}}.
\]

Since `rho_*(omega_A)` remains bounded below while `ell_A -> 0`, the right-hand side tends to zero. Equivalently,

\[
C\gtrsim \ell_A^{-1/2}=A^{p/2}\to\infty.
\]

This contradicts the existence of a universal constant `C`.

For the concrete choice `p=3`, the required constant scales as

\[
C\gtrsim A^{3/2},
\]

while the packet contributions satisfy

\[
\|\omega_{packet}\|_2\asymp A^{-7/2},
\qquad
\|\nabla\omega_{packet}\|_2\asymp A^{-1/2}.
\]

Thus the packet becomes invisible to the global scale `rho_*` while its local directional variation becomes arbitrarily severe.

## 3. What this falsifies

The attack falsifies G1* **if it is asserted as a universal statement for all smooth divergence-free initial data/solutions at arbitrary times with constants depending only on the global scale above**.

It does **not** rule out a genuinely dynamical depletion theorem valid only:

- in a blow-up regime;
- after a dynamically selected time;
- on connected intense-vorticity components;
- with local, componentwise length scales;
- or under additional hypotheses generated by the Navier–Stokes evolution.

Since every smooth divergence-free initial datum admits a local smooth solution, a universal statement including `t=0` must survive arbitrary localized high-amplitude packets. G1* in the global form does not.

## 4. Consequence for the research program

The correct next target cannot use only a single global `rho_* = kappa ||omega||_2/||grad omega||_2`.

A viable replacement has to be **local and dynamical**. One candidate architecture is:

\[
\rho_j(t)
\sim
\frac{\|\omega\|_{L^2(U_j(t))}}
{\|\nabla\omega\|_{L^2(U_j(t))}},
\]

where `U_j(t)` is a connected intense-vorticity component, together with a direction-coherence estimate within that same component. Even that remains only a conjectural architecture; it requires a theorem showing that the Navier–Stokes evolution prevents a bad component from carrying an arbitrarily small local directional scale.

Another option is to abandon pointwise Hölder coherence and target a weaker critical quantity such as a scale-local mean oscillation/BMO condition for the vorticity direction.

## 5. Relation to known geometric regularity work

The exponent `1/2` is not an arbitrary new threshold. Earlier work on vorticity-direction coherence identifies the `1/2` Hölder case as critical, and subcritical exponents `>1/2` are substantially easier. Recent work by Z. Grujic (2026) studies critical vorticity-direction regularity via decay of local mean oscillations and emphasizes the tangential strain component rather than depletion of the entire stretching term.

Therefore a final paper should not claim novelty for the mere appearance of `beta=1/2`. Novelty would have to lie in proving a new dynamical mechanism that **forces** an appropriate critical coherence condition from the Navier–Stokes equations themselves.

## 7. Next exact proof obligation

The next useful theorem must exclude the localized-packet counterexample by using genuinely dynamical information. A meaningful target is of the schematic form:

> For a putative first singular time `T*`, there exist `t0<T*`, a family of connected high-vorticity components `U_j(t)`, and scale-covariant local radii `rho_j(t)` such that the vorticity direction on each dynamically relevant component satisfies a critical coherence or mean-oscillation bound uniformly for `t in [t0,T*)`.

That is now the principal candidate bridge to attack. It is strictly stronger scientifically than the discarded global G1* because it states exactly what the Navier–Stokes dynamics would have to generate, rather than what arbitrary smooth divergence-free fields plainly need not satisfy.
